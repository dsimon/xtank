## 1. Objective

Use full entity keys and generation-stamped live input through snapshot stalls,
losses, reset and respawn, retaining the approved timing policy; guarantee that
same-owner bullet reuse, same-kind explosion reuse and vehicle lives past 255
never interpolate old/new entities; make one-shot fire/weapon edges and held
releases survive datagram loss; ensure a stale edge cannot fire after a life
change; refresh input/heartbeat at 15 Hz independently of snapshots/render FPS;
and clear held edges, redundancy and history on disconnect/reset while
command-ID scope stays correct.

## 3. Implementation

### 3.1 Full entity keys (u32)

The pre-P3.11 client matched vehicles on `(slot, u8 generation)` and matched
bullets on `(id, owner_slot, owner_generation)` and explosions on `id` alone.
Both are unsafe: a vehicle life past 255 truncated, and a same-owner bullet slot
or same-kind explosion slot reused by a new life matched the old record, so the
renderer swept from the dead entity's position to the new one (`model.c`). The
fix keys every dynamic entity by its full D3-C identity — vehicle
`(slot, generation)`, bullet `(id, owner_slot, owner_generation, generation)`,
explosion `(id, generation)`. A reused slot is therefore absent from the
previous view's key set; the old entity is dropped and the new one is snapped
(never interpolated). Interpolation, the one-tick buffer
(`XTK_MODEL_BUFFER_TICKS == 1.0`), the eight-snapshot ring and the camera are
unchanged.

### 3.2 Generation-stamped live input

Every command is stamped with `life_generation` (the controlled entity-life
generation observed from the authorized views). On a change to a new known life
the controller discards latched one-shot edges and the redundancy ring, so an
edge produced or queued under a previous life is never replayed under the new
one; held controls are restamped from the new life. `frame_emit()` feeds the
generation from `model_have_own_pose()` each frame before emitting.

### 3.3 Reliable one-shot companions and held releases

`input_emit_reliable()` walks the redundancy ring once, filling the channel-2
`INPUT_BATCH` (unchanged) and a channel-0 `RELIABLE_CMD` containing only the
commands whose loss matters: one-shot `FIRE`/`WEAPON` edges and the one-shot
`DRIVE_REL`/`FIRE_HOLD` releases (`a == 0`). Both messages carry the identical
command ids, so the server's shared cross-channel dedup (`Src/server_net.c`)
applies each at most once while a lost unreliable datagram is still recovered.
`session_client_send_input_reliable()` sends the companion reliably.

### 3.4 Stale edges after a life change

Client-side, a life change clears latched edges and the redundancy history, so a
stale edge is never emitted. Server-side, `Src/server_net.c` now validates the
non-zero `life_generation` against
`server_events_generation(XTK_ENTITY_VEHICLE, slot)` and rejects a mismatch
`XTK_ERR_STALE_LIFE`, covering an edge already in flight when the life changed
(the client cannot retract it). A zero stamp is unstamped/legacy and is admitted,
which preserves the inherited admission fixtures.

### 3.5 Independent 15 Hz input/heartbeat

`frame_emit()` emits on a wall-clock `next_emit_ns` schedule, not when a
snapshot arrives. A snapshot stall therefore cannot starve the server's
valid-input clock; the tick used is only a freshness hint and never reserves a
future engine tick. The input fixture proves the controller emits one heartbeat
per 15 Hz tick with monotonic packet sequencing using no snapshot or model at
all.

### 3.6 Disconnect/reset and command-ID scope

`input_reset()` (called on new epoch and on the results→lobby reset barrier)
and leaving `Running` clear held controls, latched edges and the redundancy
ring while `next_command_id` stays monotonic; disconnect likewise clears state.
The input fixture proves no stale edge/hold survives a disconnect and that ids
are never reused after `input_reset`.
