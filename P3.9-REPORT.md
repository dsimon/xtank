# P3.9 — Transport isolation, fragmentation and connection reuse

**Status: PASS.** Executed 2026-10-13 on the `windows-phase1` working tree
(Phase 1/2/3 owner work). The shared transport now has one nonblocking
receive/router owner with an aggregate per-tick work bound, recorded
application and ENet queue caps, D3-F unreliable snapshot fragmentation with
atomic reassembly, single-owner wrapper lifetime/reuse, and isolation of a
frozen, flooding or failed peer to itself. The frozen simulation is
byte-identical (G3 below), the Phase 1 exit gate passes and the inherited
Phase 3 drivers pass. Commits are the owner's; P3.9 committed nothing.

Contract: `docs/PHASE3-PLAN.md` §3 P3.9. Authority: **D3-F**
(`docs/PHASE3-DECISIONS.md`) — keep self-contained 15 Hz full views within the
existing 64 KiB app bound; verify pinned ENet unreliable fragmentation +
atomic reassembly, using its supported unreliable-fragment flag where needed;
no silent downgrade of an oversized view to the reliable backlog; a measured
chunking/compression proposal would come back to the owner before any wire
change. No new owner ruling emerged, so `docs/PHASE3-DECISIONS.md` was not
modified and no wire byte changed.

## 1. Objective

Make the shared transport's application and ENet work/memory bounds hold across
two peers, reconnects and maximum snapshots: one receive/router owner; an
aggregate 64-service-event budget per tick; bounded decode/command/send work;
recorded application per-peer/host and real ENet queue caps; at most one newest
unsent snapshot retained; 256 KiB/peer reliable output with a specified bounded
overshoot; a frozen reader/flood/forced send failure isolated to that peer;
maximum application snapshots reassembled atomically under fragment loss and
reorder; a packet capture proving the approved reliability choice; and ≥100
connect/disconnect/rejoin cycles reusing wrappers without stale queued packets,
slot exhaustion or growing resources while a healthy peer progresses.

## 3. Implementation

### 3.1 One receive/router owner and the aggregate budget

The plan survey flagged that `Src/server_net.c` and `Src/server_session.c` both
serviced and drained the transport. P3.9 moves every `transport_service()` and
`transport_receive()` call into `Src/server_net.c`:

- `server_net_service(tick)` is the only caller of `transport_service()`. It
  tracks `service_used` for the current `service_tick` and services only the
  remaining part of `XTK_MAX_SERVICE_WORK_PER_TICK` (64), so the aggregate is
  bounded to 64 events **per tick even when called repeatedly or from both
  paths**.
- `server_net_drain(tick, fn, ctx)` is the only caller of
  `transport_receive()`. It decodes at most `SERVER_NET_SERVICE_BUDGET` (64)
  datagrams and routes each through `fn` (the session coordinator's
  `session_rx`) or the internal INPUT_BATCH/RELIABLE_CMD admission when `fn` is
  NULL. Its per-tick high-water is recorded as `drain_max_per_tick`.

`server_session_service()` now calls those two functions; the D3-E per-peer
event quota and protocol dispatch moved into the `session_rx` callback. A
static driver check asserts `transport_service(`/`transport_receive(` appear
only in `Transport/enet_transport.c` and `Src/server_net.c`.

### 3.2 Recorded caps: application and real ENet queues

- Application host receive queue: `TRANSPORT_RX_QUEUE_MAX` (64) copies; a full
  queue drops the newest packet and counts it (`dropped_rx`), with the observed
  high-water in `TransportStats.rx_queue_max`.
- Per-peer reliable output: `XTK_MAX_RELIABLE_QUEUE_BYTES` (256 KiB). Both
  `server_net_session_backpressure()` and the session listen loop abort a peer
  that exceeds it. The overshoot is bounded to **at most one queued
  application message past the cap** (the check runs every service tick; the
  fixture measures ≤ cap + one 4 KiB message).
- Real ENet per-peer queue: `transport_peer_outgoing_bytes()` sums every
  not-yet-removed ENet command (reliable + unreliable, including
  `sentReliableCommands` and `reliableDataInTransit`), so the actual transport
  footprint is measurable independently of the application layer.

### 3.3 D3-F fragmentation and atomic reassembly

`transport_send()` keeps `ENET_PACKET_FLAG_RELIABLE` for channel 0 and now uses
`ENET_PACKET_FLAG_UNRELIABLE_FRAGMENT` for channels 1/2. Without the flag ENet
sends an oversized unreliable packet as `SEND_FRAGMENT | ACKNOWLEDGE`
(reliable) commands, silently turning a lost 64 KiB view into a retransmitted
reliable backlog. With it, ENet emits `SEND_UNRELIABLE_FRAGMENT` commands; a
packet that does not fully reassemble is discarded whole by ENet and is never
partially applied.

### 3.4 Wrapper lifetime and reuse

`pool_claim()` marks a reused slot and counts it. On an observed disconnect,
`transport_poll_disconnect()` first purges every undelivered queued copy
belonging to the wrapper, then `peer_release()` clears `enet->data` and frees
the slot. The returned pointer still maps the caller to its own peer record
until the slot is claimed again, but a new ENet peer reusing the same address
can no longer resolve to the old wrapper's connection, and the old wrapper's
queued bytes can never be delivered under a new connection.

### 3.5 At most one newest unsent snapshot

`session_deliver()` no longer departs a peer on a transient unreliable snapshot
send failure. It retains a single newest unsent snapshot (`pending_snap`,
`have_pending_snap`), retries it first on the next tick, and a fresh snapshot
replaces any older retained one. A bounded streak
(`SERVER_SESSION_SNAP_FAIL_MAX` = 30, 2 s at 15 Hz) still reclaims a genuinely
dead peer. Reliable control/event send failures keep the P3.8 fail-the-peer
behaviour.

## 5. Fixture coverage — `transport.sh` (60 checks, 3 identical repeats)

Resumed session note: the transport/session sources and the drivers were
re-audited against the contract and are unchanged from the first execution. The
only change was completing the acceptance clause's *reorder* half — the fixture
gained a deterministic fragment-reorder case (44 transport checks, up from 41)
so "atomic reassembly under fragment loss/reorder" is proven, not just asserted.

### `transport` mode (44 checks)

- **Budget/caps:** a two-peer ENet host; application rx depth capped at 64 and
  excess drops counted; aggregate service events ≤64 for each of two ticks
  across repeated calls; measured `service_max_events` and `drain_max_per_tick`
  bounded; two admission sessions; a 256 KiB/peer reliable backlog with bounded
  overshoot; the saturated session aborted alone while the healthy session
  survives and still round-trips.
- **Fragmentation/capture:** a 60 000-byte channel-1 snapshot is sent as 44
  **unreliable** fragments (capture: `datagrams=44 cmd0x0C=44 cmd0x88=0`) and
  reassembled whole on channel 1; a channel-0 snapshot is captured as
  `SEND_FRAGMENT|ACK` and reassembled whole; under deterministic fragment loss
  (fragment 2 of every other snapshot dropped) `sent=40 delivered=20
  partial=0 dropped-fragments=20` — every delivered snapshot is complete and
  byte-identical and nothing partial is ever delivered; and under deterministic
  **fragment reorder** (fragment 1 of a snapshot is held until the socket
  drains, so the peer sees it last) the 60 000-byte snapshot is still
  reassembled whole (`rr=1 rlen=60000`) and never partially applied.
- **Reuse:** 100 connect/disconnect/rejoin cycles all succeed
  (`connects=100 wrappers_released=100 wrappers_reused=99`), the pool is never
  exhausted, an undrained copy is purged on release (no stale packet reaches
  the next connection) and resources do not grow without bound.
- **Isolation:** a flooding peer is bounded by the per-peer event quota while
  the healthy peer's heartbeat is still admitted; a frozen reader's reliable
  backlog reaches the 256 KiB cap and it is aborted alone while the healthy
  peer keeps round-tripping.

### `session` mode (16 checks)

A real ENet client is admitted, handshakes, configures Combat1/Vanguard and
reads; the solo match auto-starts. With snapshot sends forced to fail, exactly
one newest unsent snapshot is retained (never more), the sent sequence does not
advance and the peer survives. When the link recovers, the retained snapshot is
flushed, no snapshot remains retained, the sequence advances and the client
receives a full `VIEW_SNAPSHOT`.

### Sandbox adaptation (test-harness only)

This sandbox's seccomp denies `sendmsg()`/`sendto()` with an explicit
destination address. `__wrap_sendmsg()` therefore connects the socket to the
destination, sends, records the (possibly auto-assigned) local address, then
disconnects and rebinds it — an equivalent kernel send path that still lets one
in-process host serve two peers. The same wrapper captures the ENet protocol
commands actually handed to the kernel (the packet-capture evidence), and
`__wrap_recvmsg()` provides the deterministic fragment-loss and
fragment-reorder simulation. This
changes no production code and no wire behaviour; on a host with unrestricted
UDP the wrappers are no-ops.

## 6. Stop-condition assessment

None triggered:

- **Hidden ENet queue growth/blocking:** the reliable backlog is capped at
  256 KiB and aborted; `transport_peer_outgoing_bytes()` measures the real ENet
  queue; the paused/frozen reader neither stalls ticks nor grows unbounded.
- **Accidental reliable snapshots:** the capture proves channel 1 uses
  `SEND_UNRELIABLE_FRAGMENT` (0x0C) and never `SEND_FRAGMENT|ACK` (0x88).
- **Partial view application:** under simulated fragment loss and reorder every
  delivered snapshot was whole and byte-identical; none partial.
- **Wrapper reuse aliases old traffic:** a released wrapper's undelivered
  copies are purged and its ENet peer unbound before the slot is reused; 100
  cycles show no stale delivery.
- **Budget exceeds approved limits:** service, decode, rx and reliable budgets
  stay within their recorded caps.

No minimal reproducer or scope question is required; the wire was not changed.
